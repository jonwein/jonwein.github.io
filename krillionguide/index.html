Overview

Krillion pays for depth. 94% of accepted answers are deep cuts worth 85 points, and the 100-point Krillion usually follows one of six patterns: the smallest member of a list, the first of its kind, the defunct one, a famous name that qualifies on a technicality, a forgotten franchise entry, or something weird with a story.

This guide covers every archived daily game, from #1 (July 16, 2026) through #130 (November 22, 2026). That is 910 prompts and 1,826,442 accepted answers, each with its rarity tier. It rebuilds every night when a new game enters Krillion's archive.

How scoring works: each day has seven “Name a…” prompts, and each answer earns points by rarity. The best day possible is 700.

What makes a Krillion

Most of the 1,113 past Krillions fit one of six patterns. Krillion's own FAQ says the gem is picked for fun as well as rarity: “the answer that's most satisfying to land on,” and one that “at least a few of you actually get.” So aim for an answer with a story, not just the most obscure thing you can type.

The technicality pattern is the easiest to miss: 78 Krillions were ordinary 10- or 30-point answers on some other prompt.

Krillions that repeat. 25 answers were the Krillion more than once, so learn these first.

  • Four times: Lesotho, Saint Kitts and Nevis
  • Three times: Brunei, Kiribati, Marshall Islands, Miracle fruit
  • Twice: Ciudad de la Paz, Comoros, Dalmatian, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Dinosaur, Dominica, Eswatini, Guinea-Bissau, Hoatzin, Kyrgyzstan, Mongolia, Oilbird, Samoa, San Marino, Seychelles, São Tomé and Príncipe, Timor-Leste, Tuvalu, Vanuatu

Locking in a deep cut (85) when the Krillion won't come

  1. Go one level down. Swap the flagship for a lesser member: Margay instead of a big cat, Comoros instead of Egypt, Bandy instead of ice hockey.
  2. Use full official names of small countries. Saint Kitts and Nevis (avg 81 over 23 prompts); Saint Vincent and the Grenadines (avg 81 over 22 prompts); Antigua and Barbuda (avg 78 over 25 prompts); São Tomé and Príncipe (avg 78 over 16 prompts).
  3. For titles, try a one-word common noun. Songs, films, brands and characters share names, so Pandora (avg 82 over 23 prompts), Merlin (avg 78 over 19 prompts), Onyx (avg 78 over 19 prompts) and Hopper (avg 79 over 17 prompts) score as deep cuts almost anywhere.
  4. Don't overshoot to the cliché. The go-to “obscure” pick scores only 15 (“too clever”): Human on 4 prompts, Tomato on 2 prompts, plus 26 one-offs such as Axolotl, Methuselah and False nine.

Study guide by topic

Study countries first: a country prompt ran on 94 of 130 days, and one memorized list of small nations covers most of them. Topics below are ordered by how many of the 910 prompts they took. Numbers in parentheses are average points on that topic's prompts where the answer was accepted.

1. Geography · 148 prompts (16%)

Comes up as: countries filtered by region, letter, flag, border, size or language; capitals; cities by size or name ending; rivers, islands, seas and parks.

Learn these countries (average points on the 113 prompts that ask for a country):

  • Pacific: Kiribati (85), Tuvalu (84), Palau (83), Marshall Islands (83), Nauru (81), Micronesia (80), Solomon Islands (75), Tonga (73)
  • Africa: Comoros (87), Guinea-Bissau (84), Eswatini (81), Lesotho (80), São Tomé and Príncipe (79), Mauritania (79), Central African Republic (79), Equatorial Guinea (77), Seychelles (75), Burkina Faso (74), Djibouti (72)
  • Caribbean: Saint Kitts and Nevis (82), Saint Vincent and the Grenadines (82), Antigua and Barbuda (78), Dominica (77), Saint Lucia (76), Grenada (75)
  • Asia: Timor-Leste (84), Tajikistan (78), Kyrgyzstan (76), Brunei (75), Turkmenistan (74)

Krillion pattern: the smallest or most-forgotten country that fits (Lesotho won four times, Saint Kitts and Nevis four times), or a big country that fits on a technicality: Japan for glaciers, the United Kingdom for a desert climate, Greece for having territories (Mount Athos).

Past Krillions: Belmopan, Funafuti, Yaren, Ciudad de la Paz, Neutral Moresnet, Bouvet Island, Sea of Marmara, Surigao Strait, Spillville.

2. Food & Drink · 114 prompts (13%)

Comes up as: dishes by country (Italian, Korean, Mexican and more), fruits and vegetables by letter or colour, sauces, candy and brands.

Learn these: obscure fruits score everywhere. Babaco, bael, petai, ackee, santol, breadnut, snake gourd, gac, tamarillo, pequi and nance each averaged 79–88 across 6–8 prompts. Miracle fruit was the Krillion three times.

Krillion pattern: weird with a story (Casu marzu, Fool's Gold Loaf, which Elvis flew to Denver to eat) or a food with a trick. Miracle fruit, which makes sour taste sweet, won three times.

Past Krillions: Su filindeu, Casu marzu, Stargazy pie, Escamoles, Buddha jumps over the wall, Bedfordshire clanger, Coco de mer.

3. Film, TV & Characters · 100 prompts (11%)

Comes up as: a studio or franchise's films or characters (Pixar, Marvel, Star Wars, Shrek), an actor's or director's films, and IMDb-rated shows.

Learn these: minor characters and early or obscure entries. Bathilda Bagshot, Syrio Forel, Pong Krell, Monica Rambeau and Casino Royale (1967) were all Krillions. Single-word titles like Pandora, Karma, Nemesis and Supernova count as deep cuts for title prompts.

Krillion pattern: the entry fans forgot (Elio, Dinosaur, Casino Royale 1967, Gabbo) or a famous person who technically qualifies (Miley Cyrus for the MCU, via an uncredited voice role). Galaxy Quest won “a movie involving time travel” for its 13-second Omega 13 device.

4. Everyday Life & Objects · 100 prompts (11%)

Comes up as: “something that…” prompts (has wheels, sold in a tube, uses steam), clothing, tools, furniture and household items.

Learn these: specialist machines fit many prompts. Carpet cleaner, street sweeper, sod cutter, brush mower, trencher, skid steer, dredger, samovar and coffee roaster each averaged 77–85 across 5–7 prompts.

Krillion pattern: the historical version of the thing (Macuahuitl, the Aztec obsidian club; Safety coffin; Dandy horse) or a technicality (an earthworm has a “saddle”; dried candlefish can be lit like a candle).

Past Krillions: Macuahuitl, Falu red, Spokeshave, Aeolipile, Safety coffin, Claw of Archimedes, Ghost station.

5. Animals & Nature · 96 prompts (11%)

Comes up as: animal groups (sharks, owls, whales), animals by letter or trait, and trees, flowers and mushrooms.

Learn these: African antelopes and small cats. Bongo, kudu, hartebeest, bushbuck, blackbuck, oryx, black-footed cat, jaguarundi, margay and sand cat each averaged 69–83 across 9–12 prompts. Also geoduck, sei whale, Bryde's whale, springhare and golden mole.

Krillion pattern: newly described or nearly lost species (Saola, found in 1992; Skywalker hoolock gibbon, named in 2017; Kouprey, possibly extinct), or an animal with one odd trick (the Hoatzin ferments leaves like a cow; the Oilbird echolocates). Hoatzin won twice and Oilbird twice.

Past Krillions: Kouprey, Saola, Hoatzin (twice), Oilbird (twice), Aye-aye, Pink fairy armadillo, Olinguito, Babirusa.

6. Brands, Tech & Work · 76 prompts (8%)

Comes up as: apps and features, brands in a category, jobs and finance terms. This topic barely existed in July and peaked in October.

Learn these: defunct or regional brands and Easter-egg products. Past Krillions include Hooters Air, TempleOS, Microsoft Comic Chat, Google Ngram Viewer, Mum (deodorant) and Code Scanner (iPhone).

Krillion pattern: it really existed but nobody remembers it (Hooters Air, Mum deodorant from 1888, WorldWideWeb, JumpStation), or it's hidden in plain sight (Code Scanner, the iPhone's hidden QR app).

7. Science, Body & Health · 63 prompts (7%)

Comes up as: elements, body parts, the solar system, minerals and units.

Learn these: rare-earth and transition elements: Erbium (89), Yttrium (88), Cerium (85), Indium (85), Scandium (83), Niobium (80), Thallium (80). For body parts: Pia mater (85), Locus coeruleus (85), Microglia (85), Trabecular meshwork (85).

Krillion pattern: tiny or newly named things, such as the Fabella (a knee bone), the Habenula, Quecto (the SI prefix for 10^-30), Gonggong (named by public vote in 2019) and STEVE (a sky glow named after a joke in Over the Hedge). Also the technicality: Gold won “an element that makes up the human body.”

Past Krillions: Stapes, Fabella, Canal of Schlemm, Habenula, Quecto, Painite, Gonggong, STEVE.

8. Sports & Fitness · 59 prompts (6%)

Comes up as: sports by trait (played with a stick, over a net, on grass), teams, champions and gym exercises.

Learn these: niche sports that fit many filters: Kalaripayattu (88), Hooverball (88), Stoolball (88), Skijoring (85), Bandy (82), Basque pelota (82), Ringette (82), Broomball (79).

Krillion pattern: a sport that once counted (Cricket and Tug of war were Olympic in 1900), a regional twist on a famous sport (Kilikiti is Samoan cricket), or a folk sport with history (Shin-kicking, contested since the 1600s).

Past Krillions: Kilikiti, Pesäpallo, Fierljeppen, Bog snorkelling, Course landaise, Prancercise.

9. Language, Words & Books · 48 prompts (5%)

Comes up as: languages by region or ending (-ese, -ish, -an), word types, and authors' books.

Learn these: small or regional languages: Manx (85), Tatar (85), Buginese (85), Comorian (81), Faroese (80), Scots (80), Hausa (80), Madurese (80).

Past Krillions: Enheduanna (the earliest known named author), Cardenio (Shakespeare's lost play), Dalmatian (a dead language), Ladino, Venustraphobia, Skeltonic verse, and Inro, spelled from the letters of “krillion”.

10. Games · 37 prompts (4%)

Comes up as: Pokémon, Mario and Nintendo, Minecraft, board and card games.

Learn these: ancient games (Senet, Pachisi, Rithmomachia, Knucklebones) and forgotten Pokémon (Porygon, Pyukumuku, Binacle, Mothim).

Krillion pattern: the first or oldest (Senet, played over 5,000 years ago; Computer Space, the first commercial arcade game) and the Pokémon nobody picks.

11. People, History & Culture · 36 prompts (4%)

Comes up as: gods by pantheon, leaders, religions and holidays.

Learn these: minor deities (Coeus, Kvasir, Cardea, Cloacina) and offbeat festivals (Up Helly Aa, Night of the Radishes).

Past Krillions: Martin Van Buren (the first president born after independence), Silbannacus (a little-known Roman emperor), Coeus, Narendra Modi (the first head of government past 100 million Instagram followers) and the Pythagorean cup.

12. Music & Dance · 33 prompts (4%)

Comes up as: an artist's songs, instruments, and genres.

Learn these: odd instruments (Wagner tuba, Ophicleide, Octobass, Daxophone) and B-sides. Generic one-word titles (Why, Home, Bones, Starlight) each averaged 80–85 across 5 prompts.

Past Krillions: Wagner tuba, Ophicleide (the tuba's 1800s ancestor), Daxophone, Octobass, The Inner Light (a Beatles B-side) and Jimmie Nicol (the Beatles' stand-in drummer).

Key statistics

Geography leads with 148 of 910 prompts (16%), and Food & Drink is second at 13%. Together with Film, TV & Characters, Everyday Life & Objects and Animals & Nature, five topics make up 61% of all prompts.

Deep cuts make up almost every answer sheet: 1,711,685 of 1,826,442 accepted answers (94%) score 85. Krillions are scarce by design, with 1,113 in total: one on 674, two on 218, three on 1, and none on 17.

Trends over time

Krillion has moved from short, fixed-topic lists to wide-open prompts. The median answer sheet grew from 65 answers in the first week to 2,376 in the latest, while the answers worth 30 points or less only grew from about 21 to 34.

For studying, that shifts where the points are. Deep cuts are easier than ever, since nearly any specific, real answer now lands at 85. The Krillion is still one answer in thousands, so the patterns in What makes a Krillion are now the main way to earn the last 15 points.

Topic slots stopped being fixed after August. Through game #47, each slot had a home topic. From game #48 on, topics rotate freely, so expect anything in any slot.
